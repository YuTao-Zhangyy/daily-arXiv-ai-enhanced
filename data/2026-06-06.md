We evaluate four models spanning open-weight and proprietary architectures (Qwen2.5:7b, Gemma2:9b, GPT-4o, GPT-5.4-mini) across three prompt designs of increasing complexity, on two human-annotated datasets of approximately 298 and 340 texts respectively. Results show that zero-shot LLMs achieve good classification performance on both tasks without any task-specific training data, establishing a practical baseline for relational annotation. Performance differs across tasks: attack detection is robust to prompt design and model choice, while praise detection is more sensitive to both, reflecting the greater subjectivity of positive relational gestures. These findings lay the groundwork for integrating LLM-based relational annotation into sign prediction pipelines.
